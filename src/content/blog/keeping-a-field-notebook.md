After [a morning with the tide pools](/blog/tide-pools/) I came home and, as usual, remembered
almost none of it precisely. The hermit crab's shell was "big" — but big compared to what? The
sculpin was "well camouflaged," which describes nothing at all.

A field notebook fixes this, but only if you write in it wrong. Not "saw a crab," but the
actual, boring specifics: time, tide height if you know it, what the animal was doing when
first noticed, what it did after. Specifics are searchable later; impressions aren't.

My current format, after a few years of drifting formats:

- Date and location, always first line
- A rough sketch, even a bad one — sketching forces you to look longer than writing does
- Three questions the observation raised, left unanswered on purpose

That last habit came from a naturalist's notebook I read years ago and can no longer find the
source for, which is itself a small argument for writing things down.
