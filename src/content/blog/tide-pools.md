---
title: What the tide leaves behind
description: A low-tide morning spent crouched over intertidal pools, and why they reward slow looking.
pubDate: 2025-03-02
tags: [observation, coast]
---

Low tide this morning was at 6:52, which meant arriving while the rock was still dark with the
night's water. The pools left behind hold a whole displaced ocean in miniature — hermit crabs
trading shells, anemones closed to fists, a sculpin so still it reads as part of the rock until
it isn't.

The temptation is to walk the shoreline quickly, covering distance. Nothing here rewards that.
The pools that look empty at a glance are rarely empty; they're just arranged for a predator's
eye, not a curious one. Sitting at the edge of one pool for ten minutes turns up more life than
scanning twenty pools in the same time.

I've started timing myself, half as a joke: how long before something moves that I assumed was
stone. The record so far is four minutes, for a chiton I'd have sworn was a barnacle-crusted
pebble.

None of this is efficient. That's more or less the point.
