---
title: The slow eye
description: Noticing is a skill with a learning curve, not a fixed trait some people have and others don't.
pubDate: 2025-04-05
tags: [practice, attention]
---

A friend who's a much better naturalist than I am put it this way: "You're not looking for the
animal. You're looking for the thing that's slightly wrong." A shadow with a shape too regular.
A leaf moving against the direction of the wind. Stillness where you'd expect small motion, or
the reverse.

That reframing changed how I move through a place. It's less about scanning for objects and
more about holding a baseline expectation of the scene and watching for deviation from it —
which means the baseline itself has to be learned first, slowly, by looking at a lot of nothing.

This is most of what [keeping a field notebook](/blog/keeping-a-field-notebook/) is actually
for. Not the record — the record is a side effect. The real function is forcing enough time in
front of a scene that the baseline has a chance to form. I noticed the same thing sitting with
[the tide pools](/blog/tide-pools/): the ten-minute mark is roughly where my eye stops cataloguing
and starts actually seeing.

I don't think this generalizes cleanly to every kind of attention. But for anything visual and
slow-moving, it's held up every time I've tested it.
