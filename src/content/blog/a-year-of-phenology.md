title: A year of phenology, so far

I didn't set out to track phenology this year — the timing of natural events across seasons —
but that's more or less what's happened by accident, one notebook entry at a time.

It started at [the tide pools](/blog/tide-pools/) in March, mostly about learning to look
properly. That habit of attention turned out to be the throughline for everything after:
[the slow eye](/blog/the-slow-eye/) piece was really just naming what the notebook had already
taught me, and [keeping a field notebook](/blog/keeping-a-field-notebook/) was the scaffolding
that made any of it repeatable instead of one nice morning I'd half-remember later.

Then the timescales started stretching. [Moth nights](/blog/moth-nights/) compressed everything
into a single evening of waiting. [Lichen as a clock](/blog/lichen-as-a-clock/) stretched it back
out to years. Laid end to end, the entries cover a genuine range — minutes to decades — using
what's basically the same three questions at the bottom of every page.

The plan for the rest of the year is simple: keep the notebook, keep measuring the lichen once,
and see what late autumn adds to the pattern that spring and summer couldn't show on their own.
