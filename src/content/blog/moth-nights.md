title: Moth nights

A white sheet, a mercury vapor bulb, and a lawn chair — mothing setups are almost comically
simple, and I wasn't prepared for how different the waiting felt compared to daylight
observation. With [the tide pools](/blog/tide-pools/), stillness reveals what's already there.
At the sheet, stillness is bait; you're not finding anything, you're being found.

The first hour was quiet enough that I doubted the setup. Then, all at once, a underwing moth
the size of my palm landed dead center and stayed for twenty minutes, wings tented, doing
nothing describable except being extraordinary to look at.

[The slow eye](/blog/the-slow-eye/) I've been trying to build during the day turned out to be
close to useless here — everything arrives already announced, wings first, no camouflage to
read past. What replaced it was a different kind of patience: waiting for arrivals instead of
scanning for the hidden.

Different terrain, same notebook, same three unanswered questions at the bottom of the page.
